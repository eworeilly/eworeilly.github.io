Research

My research develops mathematical theory to understand and improve modern data science and machine learning methods. I use tools from stochastic geometry, convex geometry, and probability theory to analyze the geometric structure of high-dimensional data and algorithms.

Geometric Structure of High-Dimensional Data

The Mondrian process in machine learning is a recursive partition of space with random axis-aligned cuts used to build random forests that achieve minimax optimal rates and Laplace kernel approximations, and it can be viewed as a special case of the stable under iterated tessellation (STIT) in stochastic geometry. My work uses the theory of stationary random tessellations to study learning algorithms based on oblique variants of the Mondrian process where linear combinations of covariates are used to generate splits.

    • Point Processes in Statistical Learning

    Point processes provide a natural framework for modeling spatial and geometric randomness. I develop applications of point process theory to problems in machine learning, including feature selection, network analysis, and spatial data analysis.

    Key topics: Determinantal point processes, Gibbs processes, spatial statistics, sampling algorithms

    Related publications: [Paper 2], [Paper 4]

    Probabilistic Foundations of Neural Networks

    Modern deep learning relies on complex optimization landscapes in extremely high dimensions. I use techniques from high-dimensional probability to understand the behavior of neural networks during training and the properties of learned representations.

    Key topics: Random matrix theory, concentration inequalities, optimization geometry, neural tangent kernels

    Related publications: [Paper 6], [Paper 7]

    Convex Optimization in Data Science

    Many machine learning problems can be formulated as convex optimization problems. I study the geometric properties of these optimization problems and develop improved algorithms using insights from convex geometry.

    Key topics: Convex relaxations, geometry of feasible regions, stochastic optimization, sparse recovery
