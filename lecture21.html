Introduction

Outline for today:

  • Stationary processes of flats;

  • Decomposition of their intensity measure;

  • Hyperplane processes;

  • Poisson hyperplane processes.

Notation:

  • \(\mathcal{A}(d,k)\): space of \(k\)-flats, i.e. \(k\)-dimensional affine subspaces of \(\mathbb{R}^d\).

  • \(\mathcal{G}(d,k)\): space of \(k\)-dimensional linear subspaces of \(\mathbb{R}^d\).

  • For \(L \in \mathcal{G}(d,k)\), \(\ell_L\) denotes Lebesgue measure on \(L\).

  • \(\kappa_d\): \(d\)-dimensional volume of the unit ball \(B^d\) in \(\mathbb{R}^d\).

\(k\)-Flat Processes

::: definition **Definition (\(k\)-Flat Processes).** A \(k\)-flat process in \(\mathbb{R}^d\) is a simple point process on \(\mathcal{F}'\) with (locally finite) intensity measure concentrated on \(\mathcal{A}(d,k)\). :::

As with particle processes, stationary \(k\)-flat processes exhibit a decomposition of their intensity measure.

::: theorem **Theorem.** Let \(\Phi\) be a stationary \(k\)-flat process in \(\mathbb{R}^d\) with locally finite intensity measure \(\ell \neq 0\) on \(\mathcal{A}(d,k)\). Then there exists a number \(\gamma \in (0,\infty)\) and a probability measure \(\mathbb{Q}\) on \(\mathcal{G}(d,k)\) such that for all measurable \(f : \mathcal{A}(d,k) \to \mathbb{R}_{+}\), \[\int_{\mathcal{A}(d,k)} f\,d\ell = \gamma \int_{\mathcal{G}(d,k)} \int_{L^{\perp}} f(L+x)\,\ell_{L^{\perp}}(dx)\, \mathbb{Q}(dL).\]

  • \(\gamma\) and \(\mathbb{Q}\) are uniquely determined by \(\ell\).

  • \(\gamma\) is called the intensity of \(\Phi\).

  • \(\mathbb{Q}\) is called the directional distribution of \(\Phi\).

:::

Let \(\pi_{0} : \bigcup_{k=1}^{d-1} \mathcal{A}(d,k) \to \bigcup_{k=1}^{d-1} \mathcal{G}(d,k)\) map any affine subspace to its translate through the origin.

Then, for any \(A \in \mathcal{B}(\mathcal{G}(d,k))\), \[\gamma\,\mathbb{Q}(A) = \frac{1}{\kappa_{d-k}} \mathbb{E}\!\left[ \Phi\!\left( \mathcal{F}_{B^{d}} \cap \pi_0^{-1}(A) \right) \right].\]

In particular, \[\gamma = \frac{1}{\kappa_{d-k}} \mathbb{E}\!\left[ \Phi\!\left( \mathcal{F}_{B^{d}} \right) \right],\] and \[\mathbb{Q}(A) = \frac{ \mathbb{E}\!\left[ \Phi\!\left( \mathcal{F}_{B^{d}}\cap \pi_0^{-1}(A) \right) \right] }{ \mathbb{E}\!\left[ \Phi\!\left( \mathcal{F}_{B^{d}} \right) \right] }.\] This highlights why \(\mathbb{Q}\) is called the directional distribution.

::: proposition **Proposition.** Let \(\Phi\) be a stationary \(k\)-flat process in \(\mathbb{R}^d\) with intensity \(\gamma\). Then, for all \(B \in \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^d)\), \[\mathbb{E}\!\left[ \sum_{E \in \Phi} \ell_{E}(B) \right] = \gamma\,\ell(B).\] :::

::: proof *Proof.* By Campbell’s theorem and the decomposition of the intensity measure \(\Lambda\), \[\mathbb{E}\!\left[ \sum_{E \in \Phi} \ell_{E}(B) \right] = \int_{\mathcal{A}(d,k)} \ell_{E}(B)\,\Lambda(dE).\]

\[= \gamma \int_{\mathcal{G}(d,k)} \int_{L^{\perp}} \ell_{L+x}(B)\,\ell_{L^{\perp}}(dx)\, \mathbb{Q}(dL)\]

\[= \gamma \int_{\mathcal{G}(d,k)} \ell(B)\,\mathbb{Q}(dL) = \gamma\,\ell(B).\]

\(\square\) :::
