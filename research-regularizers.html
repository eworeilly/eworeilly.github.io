Star Geometry + Regularizer Learning

Overview

Many problems in data analysis require recovering an unknown signal from noisy or incomplete measurements — for example, reconstructing a medical image from limited scan data, or estimating a statistical model from a small dataset. Regularization is a fundamental technique for making these problems well-posed: one adds a penalty term, called a regularizer, to the objective that encourages solutions with desired structural properties, such as sparsity or smoothness. The choice of regularizer profoundly affects the quality and interpretability of the result, yet principled methods for selecting it from data remain underdeveloped. Our research develops a geometric theory of regularizers and uses it to answer the question: given knowledge of the data distribution, what is the best regularizer to use?

The geometry of regularizers

We study the class of regularizers given by Minkowski functionals of star bodies — compact sets that include convex bodies (like ellipsoids and polytopes) as a special case, but also many nonconvex shapes. This is a broad family of functionals that capture many regularizers used in practice. By connecting these regularizers to dual Brunn-Minkowski theory we show that a single radial function derived from the data distribution acts as a kind of computational sufficient statistic: it encodes all the information needed to identify the optimal regularizer and to determine whether that regularizer is convex.

Learning regularizers from data

In practice, the data distribution is unknown, and regularizers must be learned from samples. We study critic-based loss functions, where a regularizer is trained to assign low values to likely data and high values to unlikely data. By leveraging star geometry and dual mixed volume theory, we show that these losses can be interpreted geometrically in terms of the data distribution, derive exact expressions for the optimal regularizer learned by this process in special cases, and identify which neural network architectures naturally produce regularizers from this star body class.

Robustness to distributional uncertainty

Real-world deployments often involve distributional shift: the data encountered at test time may differ from training data. We introduce a framework for distributionally robust optimal regularization, which identifies regularizers that remain effective under perturbations of the data distribution. Using convex duality, we reformulate the underlying robust optimization problem into tractable form. The resulting regularizers interpolate between memorizing the training distribution and using a uniform prior, and we show that robustness to Wasserstein-1 perturbations naturally promotes regularizers with smaller Lipschitz constants.
