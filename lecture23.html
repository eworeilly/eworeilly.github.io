Random Tessellations

::: definition **Definition (Random Tessellations).** A random tessellation in \(\mathbb{R}^d\) is a particle process \(\Phi\) such that:

  • Each \(K \in \mathrm{supp}(\Phi)\) is compact, convex, and has interior points.

  • \(\displaystyle \bigcup_{K \in \mathrm{supp}(\Phi)} K = \mathbb{R}^d\).

  • If \(K, K' \in \mathrm{supp}(\Phi)\) and \(K \neq K'\), then \(\mathrm{int}(K) \cap \mathrm{int}(K') = \varnothing\).

:::

Remarks:

  • All cells of a random tessellation are convex polytopes.

  • As a particle process, if \(\Phi\) is stationary, we can decompose the intensity measure to obtain the cell intensity \(\lambda\) and grain distribution \(\mathbb{Q}\).

Zero cells and Typical cells

As with stationary Voronoi tessellations, we study the following two random convex polytopes:

  1. Typical cell \(Z\) (only for stationary tessellations)

    • A random convex polytope with grain distribution \(\mathbb{Q}\).

    • depends on the choice of center \(C\).

  2. Zero cell \(Z_{0}\)

    • A random convex polytope in \(\mathrm{supp}(\Phi)\) containing the origin.

    • In the stationary case, this has the same distribution as the cell of \(\Phi\) containing any fixed \(x \in \mathbb{R}^d\), translated by \(-x\).

Let \(\mathcal{X}'\) be the space of non-empty closed, convex, compact subsets of \(\mathbb{R}^d\).

The distribution of the zero cell is, up to translations, the volume–weighted distribution of the typical cell.

::: theorem **Theorem.** Let \(\Phi\) be a stationary random tessellation in \(\mathbb{R}^d\) with cell intensity \(\lambda > 0\) and grain distribution \(\mathbb{Q}\). If \(f : \mathcal{X}' \to \mathbb{R}_{+}\) is measurable and translation invariant, then \[\mathbb{E}\!\left[f(Z_{0})\right] = \lambda\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[V_{d}(Z)\,f(Z)\right].\]

In particular, the distribution of \(Z_{0} - c(Z_{0})\) has density \(\lambda V_{d}(\cdot)\) with respect to the distribution of \(Z\), i.e. to \(\mathbb{Q}\). :::

  • Letting \(f \equiv 1\) gives \[\mathbb{E}\!\left[V_{d}(Z)\right] = \frac{1}{\lambda}.\]

  • \[\mathbb{E}\!\left[V_{d}(Z_{0})\right] = \lambda\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[V_{d}(Z)^{2}\right] = \frac{\mathbb{E}\!\left[V_{d}(Z)^{2}\right]}{\mathbb{E}\!\left[V_{d}(Z)\right]}.\]

::: proof *Proof.* By Campbell’s theorem, \[\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}\!\left[f(Z_{0})\right] &= \mathbb{E}\!\left[ \sum_{K \in \mathrm{supp}(\Phi)} f(K)\,\mathbf{1}_{\{0 \in \mathrm{int}(K)\}} \right] \\[0.5ex] &= \int_{\mathcal{X}'} f(K)\,\mathbf{1}_{\{0 \in \mathrm{int}(K)\}}\,\Lambda(dK) \\ &= \lambda \int_{\mathcal{X}_0} \int_{\mathbb{R}^d} f(K+x)\,\mathbf{1}_{\{0 \in K+x\}}\,dx\,\mathbb{Q}(dK) \\ &= \lambda \int_{\mathcal{X}_0} f(K) \int_{\mathbb{R}^d} \mathbf{1}_{\{0\in K+x\}}\,dx\, \mathbb{Q}(dK) \\ &= \lambda \int_{\mathcal{X}_0} f(K)\,V_{d}(K)\,\mathbb{Q}(dK) \\ &= \lambda\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[f(Z)\,V_{d}(Z)\right].\end{aligned}\]

\(\square\) :::

Poisson Hyperplane Tessellations

Let \(\widehat{\Phi}\) be a stationary Poisson hyperplane process in \(\mathbb{R}^{d}\) with intensity \(\gamma\) and spherical directional distribution \(\varphi\) on \(S^{d-1}\).

\(\widehat{\Phi} = \sum_{i \in \mathbb{Z}} \delta_{H(u_i, t_i)},\) where \(\{u_i\}_{i \in \mathbb{Z}}\) are i.i.d. random vectors on \(S^{d-1}\) with distribution \(\varphi\), and \(\{t_i\}_{i \in \mathbb{Z}}\) is a Poisson process on \(\mathbb{R}\) with intensity \(\gamma\). (\(H(u,t) := \{x \in \mathbb{R}^{d} : \langle x, u\rangle = t\}.\))

Assume \(\mathrm{supp}(\varphi)\) is not concentrated on a great subsphere. This ensures that there does not exist a line to which all hyperplanes are almost surely parallel. Then \(\widehat{\Phi}\) induces a stationary random tessellation on \(\mathbb{R}^{d}\), and the condition above ensures that all cells are bounded.

Associated Zonoid

Many parameters of the cells of a stationary Poisson hyperplane tessellation are given by quantities relating to a deterministic convex body that characterizes its intensity measure.

::: definition **Definition.** Let \(\widehat{\Phi}\) be a stationary Poisson hyperplane process with intensity \(\gamma\) and directional distribution \(\varphi\). The associated zonoid of \(\widehat{\Phi}\) is the convex body \(\Pi\) containing the origin with support function \[h(\Pi, v) = \sup_{x \in \Pi} \langle x, v\rangle = \frac{\gamma}{2} \int_{S^{d-1}} |\langle u, v\rangle|\, \varphi(du).\] :::

  • If \(\varphi\) has finite support, then \(\widehat{\Pi}\) is the Minkowski sum of a finite number of line segments centered at \(0\), i.e. a zonotope.

  • \(\mathbb{E}\!\left[\tilde{\Phi}(\mathcal{F}_{[0,V]})\right] = 2 h(\Pi, v).\)

  • \(\mathbb{E}\!\left[V_{d}(Z)\right] = \frac{1}{\lambda} = \frac{1}{V_{d}(\Pi)}.\)

  • \(\mathbb{E}\!\left[V_{d}(Z_{0})\right] = 2^{-d} d!\,V_{d}(\Pi^{\circ}),\) where \(\Pi^{\circ} := \{x \in \mathbb{R}^{d} : \langle x, y\rangle \le 1 \ \forall y \in \Pi\}\).

  • \(f_0(Z_0)\) is the number of vertices of zero cell: \[\mathbb{E}\!\left[f_{0}(Z_{0})\right] = 2^{-d} d!\,V_{d}(\Pi^{\circ})\,V_{d}(\Pi).\] \[\mathbb{E}\!\left[f_{0}(Z_{0})\right] = 2^{-d} d!\,V_{d}(\Pi^{\circ})\,V_{d}(\Pi).\]

\[\mathbb{E}\!\left[f_{0}(Z_{0})\right] = 2^{-d} d!\,V_{d}(\Pi^{\circ})\,V_{d}(\Pi).\]
